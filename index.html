I · The premise

A wedding is not a production. It is a sacrament — and everything else, the linen and the light and the long table under the trees, exists to make that visible.

II · The offering

Five things we take off your hands

01/05

01

The Liturgy

The rite itself, planned properly. We sit with you and your celebrant and build the Mass from the books outward — the Order of Celebrating Matrimony, or the traditional Latin rite of 1962. Never from a template.

  • Modern or traditional Latin rite, within Mass or outside it
  • Readings, psalm and the form of consent
  • Nuptial blessing and Marian devotion
  • Ministers, servers and processional order
  • A printed worship aid your guests can follow

02

Sacred Music

Music that belongs in a church. We engage the singers, agree the programme with the parish director, and make sure every piece has a home in the rite.

  • Chant propers and the Ordinary — Missa de Angelis and up
  • Renaissance polyphony — Palestrina, Victoria, Byrd
  • Latin–English booklets guests can actually follow
  • Organ, schola, cantor or string ensemble
  • Parish approval and rehearsal

03

The Church

Everything inside the church doors: the sacristan, the florist, the candles, the photographer’s brief. Sanctuaries have rules. We learn them before we design anything.

  • Parish and sacristy liaison
  • Sanctuary and nave flowers
  • Candles, linens and vesture
  • Photography and film protocol

04

The Feast

Cana was a wedding feast, and a good one. We plan the reception with the same care as the rite — and with the same instinct for restraint.

  • Venue search and negotiation
  • Menu, wine and the toast
  • Table plan, stationery and calligraphy
  • Full day-of direction

05

Preparation

The paperwork nobody warns you about. We keep the canonical and civil files moving so that neither the diocese nor the State is waiting on you.

  • Pre-nuptial enquiry with your parish
  • Baptismal and confirmation certificates
  • Accord or diocesan preparation course
  • Dispensations and delegations where needed
  • Civil notification — the three-month rule

IV · The rite

What actually happens at the altar

Most couples have been to a hundred weddings and had the rite explained to them at none of them. Here is the shape of it.

I Introitus

The Entrance

The couple may process together, or the bride may be accompanied. The Church is content either way. What matters is that the procession is a procession — deliberate, sung, and headed somewhere.

II Liturgia verbi

The Liturgy of the Word

Nine first readings, seven psalms, fourteen epistles, ten gospels. You choose. We will tell you which ones your celebrant has heard four hundred times, and which one will make your grandmother cry.

III Consensus

The Consent

The heart of it. You are the ministers of this sacrament, not the priest — he is the Church’s witness. Two forms of words are provided; either may be spoken or answered. It takes about ninety seconds.

IV Benedictio nuptialis

The Nuptial Blessing

After the Our Father, the priest prays over you a blessing that is older than every building it has ever been said in. Three forms exist. Read all three before you choose; they are not interchangeable.

V Sub tuum praesidium

The Marian Devotion

Not part of the rite, and almost universal in Ireland: the bride brings flowers to Our Lady’s altar while the schola sings. A small, unhurried minute. Do not let anyone rush it.
