I · What we believe

Most wedding planning begins at the reception and works backwards, treating the ceremony as the thing that has to happen before the good part. We think that is exactly the wrong way round, and we think couples know it — which is why so many of them describe their own wedding day as a blur.

A nuptial Mass is a piece of theatre in the oldest and best sense: it has a shape, a rhythm, a text and a silence, and it repays attention. Plan it well and the rest of the day inherits its seriousness. Plan it badly — or not at all — and no amount of peonies will make up the difference.

So we start there. We read the rite with you. We choose the readings before we choose the linens. And then we design outward from the altar until we reach the last table at the back of the room.

II · Four commitments

What we hold ourselves to

Not a mission statement. Four things you may hold us to, and complain about if we miss.

{{icon:chalice|emblem-card__icon}}

Reverence

Reverentia

We will never ask a parish to bend a liturgical norm for the sake of a photograph. If something cannot be done, we will tell you early and propose something better.

{{icon:lily|emblem-card__icon}}

Beauty

Pulchritudo

Beauty is not decoration. It is how a sacrament becomes legible to people who have not been inside a church in fifteen years. We take it that seriously.

{{icon:laurel|emblem-card__icon}}

Hospitality

Hospitalitas

Half your guests will not be Catholic and some will not be believers. They should leave having understood what happened and having been fed extremely well.

{{icon:rings|emblem-card__icon}}

Fidelity

Fidelitas

To the rite, to the parish, to your budget and to the number we quoted you in February. We take no commission from any supplier we recommend.

IV · How we got here

Twelve years, more or less

MMXIV

One wedding, entirely by accident

Máire agreed to organise the music for a friend’s wedding in Galway and ended up organising the whole rite. Four more couples asked before the year was out.

MMXVII

The first worship aid

We printed a proper booklet — the full order, the texts, the music, and a short note explaining what a nuptial blessing is. Guests kept them. We have printed one for every wedding since.

MMXX

Twelve weddings, six people each

The year of restrictions taught us more about the rite than the six before it. Stripped of everything else, a wedding is still a wedding. We have planned differently ever since.

MMXXII

Rome

Our first wedding abroad, at the Pontifical Irish College. We now take three or four destination weddings a year, and no more.

Today

Eighteen weddings a year

A deliberate ceiling. It is the number at which we can still be in the sacristy at eight in the morning for every single one.
